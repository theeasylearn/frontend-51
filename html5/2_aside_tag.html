Pav bhaji

Pav Bhaji is a popular street-style Indian dish that combines a spicy, thick vegetable curry (bhaji) with buttered bread rolls (pav). The bhaji is made from boiled and mashed potatoes, cauliflower, and green peas, cooked with finely chopped onions, tomatoes, and capsicum in a mixture of butter and oil. The dish gets its distinctive flavor from pav bhaji masala, red chilli powder, turmeric, and ginger-garlic paste. The vegetables are thoroughly mashed to create a smooth, thick consistency and simmered for 8-10 minutes. The finishing touches include lemon juice, fresh coriander, and a generous knob of butter on top. The pav is prepared by slitting the bread rolls horizontally and toasting them on a hot tawa with butter until they achieve a golden, crisp exterior. This dish is served hot with the bhaji topped with extra butter, chopped raw onions, and a lemon wedge on the side, making it perfect for dinner or weekend gatherings.

Ingredients

  • 3 medium potatoes (boiled & mashed)
  • 1 cup cauliflower (finely chopped, boiled)
  • ½ cup green peas (boiled)
  • 1 large onion (finely chopped)
  • 1 large tomato (finely chopped)
  • 1 green capsicum (finely chopped)
  • 2 tbsp butter (plus extra for serving)
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 2 tsp pav bhaji masala
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder (adjust to taste)
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Fresh coriander (chopped)
  • 6–8 pav (bread rolls)

Methods


Heat butter and oil together in a pan. Add chopped onions and sauté until light golden.
Add ginger-garlic paste and cook for a minute until raw smell disappears.
Add capsicum and sauté for 2–3 minutes until slightly soft.
Add tomatoes, turmeric, chilli powder, pav bhaji masala, and salt. Cook until the mixture turns soft
and oil starts separating.
Add boiled potatoes, cauliflower, and peas. Mash everything well using a potato masher.
Add a little water to get a thick but smooth consistency. Simmer for 8–10 minutes, stirring often.
Finish with lemon juice and fresh coriander. Add a knob of butter on top.

Pav Preparation

Slit pav horizontally.
Toast on a hot tawa with butter until golden and crisp.

Serving

Serve hot bhaji topped with butter, chopped onion, lemon wedge, and toasted pav on the side. This version is street-style, rich, and full of flavor—perfect for dinner or a weekend treat.F

Masala Dosa

Masala Dosa is a quintessential South Indian dish featuring a crispy, golden crepe made from fermented rice and lentil batter, filled with a spiced potato mixture. The dosa batter requires advance preparation by soaking dosa rice, urad dal, and fenugreek seeds for 6-8 hours, then grinding them into a smooth batter and fermenting overnight. The potato masala filling is prepared by tempering mustard seeds, cumin seeds, curry leaves, and green chillies in oil, followed by sautéed onions, turmeric powder, and mashed boiled potatoes, finished with fresh coriander. To make the dosa, the fermented batter is spread thinly in a circular motion on a heated tawa, drizzled with oil around the edges, and cooked until golden and crisp on medium heat. The potato masala is placed in the center, and the dosa is folded over it. Best served hot with coconut chutney and sambar, this dish is perfect for breakfast or a light dinner.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dosa rice
  • ½ cup urad dal
  • ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds
  • Salt to taste
  • Oil or ghee for cooking dosa
  • 3 medium potatoes (boiled & mashed)
  • 1 large onion (thinly sliced)
  • 2 green chillies (finely chopped)
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 8–10 curry leaves
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Fresh coriander (chopped)

Methods


Wash and soak rice, urad dal, and fenugreek seeds for 6–8 hours.
Grind to a smooth batter, add salt, and ferment overnight.
Heat oil in a pan. Add mustard seeds and let them crackle.
Add cumin seeds, curry leaves, green chillies, and onions. Sauté until soft.
Add turmeric powder and mashed potatoes. Mix well and cook for 2–3 minutes.
Add chopped coriander and keep aside.
Heat a tawa and pour a ladle of dosa batter. Spread thin in circular motion.
Drizzle oil around edges and cook until golden and crisp.
Place potato masala in the center and fold the dosa.

Dosa Preparation

Use medium heat for crisp dosa.
Spread batter evenly and avoid thick layers.

Serving

Serve hot masala dosa with coconut chutney and sambar. Perfect for breakfast or light dinner.

Paneer Butter Masala

Paneer Butter Masala is a rich and creamy Punjabi vegetarian curry featuring soft cubes of paneer in a luxurious tomato-based gravy. The dish begins by heating a combination of butter and oil, then sautéing ginger-garlic paste followed by onion puree until golden. Tomato puree is added along with a blend of spices including red chilli powder, turmeric powder, garam masala, and coriander powder, cooked until the oil separates from the masala, indicating proper cooking. Cubed paneer is gently mixed into this flavorful gravy, followed by fresh cream which gives the dish its signature smooth, velvety texture. The curry is simmered for about 5 minutes to allow the flavors to meld together. Fresh coriander is used as garnish to add a burst of freshness. For the best results, fresh paneer should be used. This indulgent dish pairs beautifully with naan, roti, or jeera rice, making it a favorite in Indian restaurants and homes alike.

Ingredients

  • 200 g paneer (cubed)
  • 2 medium tomatoes (pureed)
  • 1 medium onion (pureed)
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • 2 tbsp fresh cream
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander (chopped)

Methods


Heat oil and butter in a pan.
Add ginger-garlic paste and sauté lightly.
Add onion puree and cook till golden.
Add tomato puree, spices, and salt. Cook until oil separates.
Add paneer cubes and mix gently.
Add cream and simmer for 5 minutes.
Garnish with fresh coriander.

Tips

Use fresh paneer for best taste.

Serving

Serve hot with naan, roti, or jeera rice.

Punjabi Chole Masala

Punjabi Chole Masala is a robust, flavorful chickpea curry that's a staple of North Indian cuisine. The dish starts with chickpeas that have been soaked overnight and boiled until tender. The cooking process begins by heating oil and tempering cumin seeds, followed by sautéing finely chopped onions until they turn golden brown, which develops a deep, caramelized flavor base. Ginger-garlic paste is added briefly, then tomato puree is incorporated along with chole masala, red chilli powder, turmeric powder, and salt. This masala mixture is cooked well until the oil begins to separate. The boiled chickpeas are then added and mixed thoroughly with the spiced gravy. A little water is added to achieve the desired consistency, and the curry is simmered for 10 minutes, allowing the chickpeas to absorb all the aromatic flavors. The dish is garnished with fresh coriander. Slow cooking enhances the flavor significantly. Chole Masala is traditionally served hot with bhature, kulcha, or steamed rice.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup chickpeas (soaked overnight & boiled)
  • 2 medium onions (finely chopped)
  • 2 medium tomatoes (pureed)
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1½ tsp chole masala
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander (chopped)

Methods


Heat oil in a pan and add cumin seeds.
Add onions and sauté until golden brown.
Add ginger-garlic paste and cook briefly.
Add tomato puree, spices, and salt. Cook well.
Add boiled chickpeas and mix.
Add little water and simmer for 10 minutes.
Garnish with fresh coriander.

Tips

Slow cooking enhances flavor.

Serving

Serve hot with bhature, kulcha, or steamed rice.
